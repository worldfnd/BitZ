\section{Overview and interfaces}
\label{sec:overview}

\begingroup\small

\subsection{The ZeroCheck-to-PCS boundary}
\label{sec:public-objects}

Let $\bm x$ be the public input and let $\bm f$ be a bounded witness.  An
admitted source relation expresses its constraints as polynomial equations
(PESAT) or as polynomial ideal-membership statements (PIMSAT):
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathsf{PESAT}:&\quad p_i(\bm x,\bm f)=0 &&(\forall i),\\
  \mathsf{PIMSAT}:&\quad p_j^{(a)}(\bm x,\bm f)\in I_a
    &&(\forall a,j).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:source-relations}
\end{equation}
The polynomial ring, bounded witness domain, and ideals in
Equation~\eqref{eq:source-relations} belong to the source relation.  The fixed
profile selects that relation and supplies the black-box ZeroCheck contract
below.  Here ``ZeroCheck'' names the source-to-linear interface; a PIMSAT
instantiation may perform ideal-membership checks internally.

Let $M$ be the native domain of one witness entry and let $R$ be the scalar
evaluation domain.  ZeroCheck declares how the verifier derives
an additive map
\begin{equation}
 \psi:M\longrightarrow R,
 \qquad \psi(x+x')=\psi(x)+\psi(x').
 \label{eq:evaluation-map}
\end{equation}
For an integer witness, $\psi$ may be reduction into $R$; for a polynomial
witness, it may combine coefficient reduction with evaluation at a public or
transcript-derived point.  In either case, $\psi(\bm f)$ means that $\psi$ is
applied entry by entry.

ZeroCheck must reduce its source claim to
\begin{equation}
 \left\langle\bm v,\psi(\bm f)\right\rangle_R
 :=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}v_i\psi(f_i)=\mu,
 \qquad \bm v\in R^r,\quad \mu\in R.
 \label{eq:zerocheck-linear-claim}
\end{equation}
The verifier derives $\psi$, $\bm v$, and $\mu$ from the ZeroCheck transcript.
The same witness $\bm f$ is retained.  Equation~
\eqref{eq:zerocheck-linear-claim} is the only source claim consumed by
the F2Z core.

\subsection{Committing the witness as bits}
\label{sec:bit-basis}

The ZeroCheck protocol fixes an ordered basis
$(\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_{D_M-1})$ for the native witness domain $M$.  Each
$\gamma_j$ is the $j$-th public basis element: every accepted $f_i$ has unique
coordinates in these elements, and the order of the indices $j$ fixes how
those coordinates are serialized.  The basis is not computed from the witness
or sampled during the protocol.  For the concrete integer case, $M=\Z$ and
the basis is simply $D_M=1$, $\gamma_0=1$.  For a polynomial case with
$M=\Z[X]_{<d}$, the usual choice is $D_M=d$ and $\gamma_j=X^j$, ordered from
the constant term through $X^{d-1}$.  Every accepted entry then has a
canonical, fixed-width decomposition
\begin{equation}
 f_i=\sum_{k=0}^{B-1}\sum_{j=0}^{D_M-1}
       b_{i,k,j}\,(2^k\gamma_j),
 \qquad b_{i,k,j}\in\bitsset,
 \qquad \bm b:=\bits(\bm f).
 \label{eq:overview-bit-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
The statement fixes the valid bit strings and the order in which the triples
$(i,k,j)$ are flattened.  Every coefficient vector below uses that same order.

F2Z commits to these bits before the first ZeroCheck challenge.  Consecutive
blocks are packed in an ordered basis of a binary extension field, encoded by
the proof system's linear code, and Merkle committed:
\begin{equation}
 P:=\Pack(\pi_2(\bm b)),\qquad
 O:=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(P),\qquad
 \rho:=\Root(O).
 \label{eq:overview-commitment}
\end{equation}
The oracle and root in Equation~\eqref{eq:overview-commitment} are carried
through ZeroCheck and the F2Z proof.  The equality in
Equation~\eqref{eq:zerocheck-linear-claim}
remains an equality in $R$; only the committed representation of the witness
lives over the binary field.

\subsection{Bitify and the role of GKR}

Bitify rewrites the ZeroCheck output directly in terms of the committed
bits.  The bit $b_{i,k,j}$ inherits the coefficient
\begin{equation}
 u_{i,k,j}:=v_i\,\psi(2^k\gamma_j).
 \label{eq:overview-bitify-coefficient}
\end{equation}

Substituting the decomposition in
Equation~\eqref{eq:overview-bit-reconstruction} into the linear claim gives
the following identity.  In the concrete proof system, $R=\Fq$ and bits enter $R$
through $\pi_q$ coordinatewise:
\begin{equation}
 \left\langle\bm v,\psi(\bm f)\right\rangle_R=\mu
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \left\langle\pi_q(\bm b),\bm u\right\rangle_R=\mu.
 \label{eq:overview-bitify}
\end{equation}
This Bitify step sends no message and creates no new commitment.

The remaining problem is to prove the claim in Equation~
\eqref{eq:overview-bitify}, which is evaluated in $R$, using bits
committed over a different field.  F2Z tensors the bit vector and lifts one
coefficient factor to bounded nonnegative integers $\lambda_i$.  For a bit
matrix $\Bbit$, a column sum has the form
\begin{equation}
 \mu_c:=\sum_i\lambda_i\Bbit[c,i]\in\Z.
 \label{eq:overview-column-sum}
\end{equation}
If the sum in Equation~\eqref{eq:overview-column-sum} lies in an accepted range
narrower than the order of $g\in\K^\times$, then exponentiation is injective on
that range and
\begin{equation}
 g^{\mu_c}
  =\prod_i g^{\lambda_i\Bbit[c,i]}
  =\prod_i\bigl(1+(g^{\lambda_i}-1)\Bbit[c,i]\bigr)
 \qquad\text{in }\K.
 \label{eq:overview-grand-product}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:overview-grand-product} turns the exact column sums into
grand-product claims over the binary field.  GKR reduces the batched product
computation to a linear opening claim on the same committed bits---an MLE
evaluation in the proof system's fixed specialization.  The binary-field opening
protocol then checks that terminal claim against $O$.

\subsection{MLE handoff and concrete proof system}
\label{sec:opening-statements}

The concrete proof system first flattens the source witness into bounded
integer cells.  At the PCS boundary it specializes the generic interface as
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  r&=2^{t+s},& M&=\Z,& \psi&=\pi_q,& D_M&=1,& \gamma_0&=1,& B&=W,\\
  f_{c,b}&:=\INT(D(b,c)),&&
  \bm f&:=\bigl(f_{c,b}\bigr)_{c\in\bitsset^s,\,b\in\bitsset^t}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:concrete-lmfb-specialization}
\end{equation}
The order in Equation~\eqref{eq:concrete-lmfb-specialization} is $c$-major,
then $b$-major; the bit order is $(c,b,j)$.  The coefficient at position
$(c,b)$ is denoted $v_{c,b}$.  This adapter is part of ZeroCheck
and must preserve the root from Equation~\eqref{eq:overview-commitment}.

For $z\in\bitsset^a$ and $\bm r\in\Fq^a$, define the equality polynomial
\begin{equation}
 \eq(z,\bm r):=\prod_{h=0}^{a-1}
   \bigl((1-z_h)(1-\bm r_h)+z_h\bm r_h\bigr).
 \label{eq:equality-polynomial}
\end{equation}
The coordinate order in Equation~\eqref{eq:equality-polynomial} is the order
fixed by the tensor layout below.

The concrete proof system instantiates the scalar handoff with an MLE evaluation.
Let $D:\bitsset^t\times\bitsset^s\to[0,2^W)$ be the committed witness tensor,
and let $r_1\in\Fq^t$ and $r_2\in\Fq^s$ be transcript-derived.  Set
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  y&:=\MLE{\pi_q\circ\INT(D)}(r_1,r_2),\\
  w_b&:=\operatorname{can}_q(\eq(b,r_1)),
  &w'_c&:=\eq(c,r_2).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:mle-opening}
\end{equation}
Expanding the MLE in Equation~\eqref{eq:mle-opening} gives the separable form
used by this proof system:
\begin{equation}
 y=\sum_{c\in\bitsset^s}w'_c\,
   \pi_q\!\left(\sum_{b\in\bitsset^t}
      w_b\,\INT(D(b,c))\right).
 \label{eq:weighted-opening}
\end{equation}
This expansion also fixes the coefficient of each committed bit:
\begin{equation}
 u_{c,b,j}:=\pi_q(w_b2^j)w'_c,
 \qquad i:=(b\ll w)\mid j.
 \label{eq:weighted-bit-coeff}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:mle-opening}--\eqref{eq:weighted-bit-coeff} give the tensor
expansion of a single MLE claim.  The table records the resulting notation and
concrete proof-system bounds.
