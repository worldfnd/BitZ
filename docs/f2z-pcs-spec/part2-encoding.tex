\section{Relations, reductions, and proof objects}
\label{sec:encoding}

\begingroup\footnotesize

\subsection{How to read this section}

Section~\ref{sec:overview} described the main F2Z reduction informally.  This
section names its intermediate claims and the reductions between them.  The
reductions appear in dependency order.

Throughout the direct path, the encoded oracle $O$ and its public root $\rho$
remain fixed.  What changes is the public claim attached to that committed
data.  Bitify also changes the witness view from the native entries $\bm f$ to
their canonical bits $\bm b=\bits(\bm f)$.

An encoded relation contains triples $(x,O;\mathsf{wit})$: $x$ is the explicit
instance, $O$ is the implicit encoded oracle, and $\mathsf{wit}$ is the
witness.  For a public witness encoder $E_{\rm wit}$, the binding condition is
\begin{equation}
 O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(E_{\rm wit}(\mathsf{wit})).
 \label{eq:encoded-oracle-binding}
\end{equation}
Merkle compilation publishes the public root $\rho:=\Root(O)$ for this oracle;
$\rho$ is not a proof field.
Every relation below is additionally parameterized by the fixed public
context $\chi:=(\mathsf{pp},t,s,W,g)$ and its derived dimensions.  The context
is omitted from displayed instances and is carried unchanged by every edge.
The three protocol interfaces used below are $\Pi_{\rm ZC}$,
$\Pi_{\rm red}:=\Pi_{\rm GKR}$, and
$\Pi_{\rm iopp}:=\Pi_{\rm open}$.

The edge notation is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal R_0\equiv_F\mathcal R_1
    &\quad\text{denotes deterministic retyping},\\
  \mathcal R_0\xrightarrow{F}\mathcal R_1
    &\quad\text{denotes deterministic compilation},\\
  \mathcal R_0\xRightarrow{\Pi}\mathcal R_1
    &\quad\text{denotes an interactive reduction},\\
  \mathcal R_0\mathrel{\dashrightarrow}\mathcal R_1
    &\quad\text{denotes a conditional extension or classification only}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:reduction-edge-notation}
\end{equation}
A proof fragment lists transmitted values only; challenges, successor
coefficients, exponentiated values, batching coefficients, and work tables are
verifier-derived.

\subsection{ZeroCheck black-box handoff}

The reduction chain starts with ZeroCheck.  Let $\mathcal R_{\rm src}$ be the
PESAT or PIMSAT source relation selected by the fixed profile.  F2Z treats its
ZeroCheck protocol as a black box and assumes only the handoff below.  The
deterministic $\mathsf{Encode}$ step binds the witness to the encoded oracle:
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_{\rm src}^{\rm enc}:=
 \Bigl\{(x_{\rm src},O;\bm f):\;&
  (x_{\rm src};\bm f)\in\mathcal R_{\rm src},
  \quad\bm f\in\mathcal W_B,\notag\\[-2pt]
 &O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}\!\left(
       \Pack(\pi_2(\bits(\bm f)))\right)\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-source-encoded}
\end{align}
This encoded relation is the sole source consumed by the PCS.

ZeroCheck supplies one black-box reduction
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{\rm src}^{\rm enc}
 \xRightarrow{\Pi_{\rm ZC}}
 \RLMFB.
 \label{eq:edge-zerocheck-handoff}
\end{equation}
The proof fragment $\pi_{\rm ZC}$ and all transcript events on this edge
belong to ZeroCheck.  Its verifier derives $(\psi,\bm v,\mu)$ and
the required separable tuple $(\bm w,\bm w',y)$ with $y=\mu$.  The adapter must
satisfy Equation~\eqref{eq:concrete-lmfb-specialization} and
$v_{c,b}=\pi_q(w_b)w'_c$, while retaining the same witness, oracle $O$, and
root $\rho$.  An oracle mismatch or incomplete event ledger causes rejection.
Once this reduction succeeds, the source constraints have been discharged;
the F2Z core receives only the derived linear claim.  For a PIMSAT source,
ZeroCheck may perform ideal-membership checks internally.

\subsection{The Lin-ModFieldBin relation}

The ZeroCheck handoff leaves a scalar linear claim about native witness
entries.  The Lin-ModFieldBin relation records that claim together with the
same encoded oracle.  Its public ordered basis and statement-bound injection
$\bits:\mathcal W_B\hookrightarrow\bitsset^n$ give the fixed reconstruction
\begin{equation}
 f_i=\sum_{k<B}\sum_{j<D_M}b_{i,k,j}2^k\gamma_j,
 \qquad b_{i,k,j}\in\bitsset,
 \qquad \bm b=\bits(\bm f).
 \label{eq:module-bit-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
This equation fixes both reconstruction and bit order.  The additive map
$\psi:M\to R$ is instantiated by ZeroCheck and carried explicitly in
the public successor instance.
\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
The resulting module relation is
\begin{align}
 \RLMFB:=\Bigl\{((\psi,\bm v,\mu),O;\bm f):\;&
 \bm f\in\mathcal W_B,
 \quad\psi:M\to R\text{ is ZeroCheck-admitted and additive},
 \quad\bm v\in R^r,
 \quad\mu\in R,
 \quad O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}\!\left(
                 \Pack(\pi_2(\bits(\bm f)))\right),\notag\\[-2pt]
 &\sum_i v_i\psi(f_i)=\mu\quad\text{in }R\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-lmfb}
\end{align}
This is the exact source-to-core seam.  The first core reduction simply
rewrites the claim using the canonical bits already bound to $O$.

\paragraph{Direct reductions.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Reduction 1: Deterministic Bitify.}

\begin{equation}
 \RLMFB
 \equiv_{\mathsf{Bitify}}
 \RLBD,
 \qquad
 u_{i,k,j}:=
 v_i\,\psi(2^k\gamma_j).
 \label{eq:edge-bitify}
\end{equation}
Bitify transmits nothing: it retypes the witness as
$\bm b:=\bits(\bm f)$ and derives the public coefficients.  Since $O$ already
binds these canonical bits, no new Booleanity test is needed.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Lin-BitsDual relation}

After Bitify, the claim is expressed directly on the committed bits.  The
corresponding core relation is
\begin{align}
 \RLBD:=\Bigl\{((\bm u,\mu),O;\bm b):\;&
 \bm b\in\operatorname{im}(\bits)\subseteq\bitsset^n,
 \quad\bm u\in R^n,
 \quad\mu\in R,
 \quad O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(\Pack(\pi_2(\bm b))),\notag\\[-2pt]
 &\langle\pi_q(\bm b),\bm u\rangle_R=\mu\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-lbd}
\end{align}
Here $\pi_q(\bm b)$ denotes the coordinatewise bit embedding into
$R=\Fq$.  Restricting $\bm b$ to $\operatorname{im}(\bits)$ makes Bitify an
equivalence rather than a one-way reduction.
Let $\iota_{2\to\K}:\Ftwo\hookrightarrow\K$ denote the corresponding
coordinatewise embedding into the binary field.

The claim now concerns the committed bits, but it is still evaluated in
$R=\Fq$, whereas the oracle proof operates over $\K$.  To cross this boundary,
reshape the bits as $\Bbit[c,i]$ and write the public coefficient in the same
$c$-major order as $u_{c,i}=u_c^{(2)}u_i^{(1)}$.  With canonical integer
representatives $\lambda_i$ of $u_i^{(1)}$, the prover exposes the column
values
\begin{equation}
 \mu_c:=\sum_i\lambda_i\Bbit[c,i]\in\Z.
 \label{eq:paper-column-values}
\end{equation}
The verifier first checks that these values reconstruct the original scalar
claim:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_c u_c^{(2)}\,\pi_q(\mu_c)=\mu\quad\text{in }R=\Fq.
 \label{eq:pi1-paper-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
It then derives $y_i:=g^{\lambda_i}$ and $\nu_c:=g^{\mu_c}$.  The bounded
integer sums become the binary-field product claims
\begin{equation}
 \prod_i\bigl(1+(y_i-1)\Bbit[c,i]\bigr)=\nu_c
 \qquad(\forall c).
 \label{eq:paper-grand-product}
\end{equation}
These are the product claims consumed by $\Pi_1$.

\paragraph{Direct reductions.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Reduction 1: Produce the F2Z encoded PESAT claim.}
\begin{equation}
 \RLBD
 \xRightarrow{\Pi_1}
 \RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z},\mathcal C,\Pack).
 \label{eq:edge-pi1}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Proof:} The prover sends $\pi_1=(\mu_c)_c$.  The verifier checks
 Equation~\eqref{eq:pi1-paper-reconstruction} before accepting the successor.
 \item \textbf{Result:} the verifier derives $\bm y=(g^{\lambda_i})_i$,
 $\bm\nu=(g^{\mu_c})_c$, and the PESAT instance; the bit witness and $O$
 are unchanged.
 \item \textbf{Scope:} the first core F2Z reduction.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Proof-system restriction: weighted coefficient shape.}
The chunked mod-$q$ proof system admits only coefficients of the form
\begin{equation}
 u_{c,b,j}=\pi_q(w_b2^j)w'_c,
 \qquad
 0\le w_b<2^{q_{\rm bits}},\qquad w'_c\in\Fq.
 \label{eq:weighted-coefficient-shape}
\end{equation}
This weighted form is the admissible public coefficient shape.

\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Active-chunk instantiation of
  \texorpdfstring{$\Pi_1$}{Pi1}}

The fixed mod-$q$ proof system implements the same $\Pi_1$ reduction in chunks,
keeping every exponent inside the injective range of $g$.  For the admitted
weighted shape, write
\begin{equation}
 w_b=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}2^{c_w\ell}\omega_b^{(\ell)},
 \qquad
 u_c^{(\ell)}:=\sum_b\omega_b^{(\ell)}\INT(D(b,c)).
 \label{eq:active-chunk-values}
\end{equation}
The verifier derives
$\mathcal A:=\{\ell<L:\exists b,\ \omega_b^{(\ell)}\ne0\}$ from the public
weights, requires $\mathcal A\ne\varnothing$, and processes its elements in
increasing order.  The values in
Equation~\eqref{eq:active-chunk-values} determine the proof fragment
\begin{equation}
 \pi_1^{\rm chunk}
 :=\bigl(\nu^{(\ell)},u^{(\ell)}\bigr)_{
       \ell\in\mathcal A\uparrow},
 \qquad
 \begin{aligned}
  u^{(\ell)}&=(u_c^{(\ell)})_{c\in\bitsset^s},\\
  \nu^{(\ell)}&=(g^{u_c^{(\ell)}})_{c\in\bitsset^s}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:pi1-chunk-proof}
\end{equation}
This tuple lists exactly the transmitted chunk fields.  For each active chunk,
$\nu^{(\ell)}$ is a NARG record
and $u^{(\ell)}$ is a checked, non-absorbed hint.  Inactive chunks have the
derived value $u^{(\ell)}=0$.  The verifier rejects a missing, duplicate,
out-of-order, or noncanonical active pair; requires
$0\le u_c^{(\ell)}<2^{127}$; and checks
$g^{u_c^{(\ell)}}=\nu_c^{(\ell)}$ before its next squeeze.  Because
$\operatorname{ord}(g)=2^{128}-1$, the stated range makes the accepted exponent
unique.  The checked hints reconstruct the original column values:
\begin{equation}
 \mu_c
 :=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L-1}2^{c_w\ell}u_c^{(\ell)}
 =\sum_b w_b\INT(D(b,c)).
 \label{eq:chunk-reconstructs-mu}
\end{equation}
After receiving the final active vector, the verifier checks
\begin{equation}
 \sum_c w'_c\,\pi_q(\mu_c)=y.
 \label{eq:pi1-weighted-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
This chunked reconstruction discharges the scalar claim before the opening
phase.

For each active $\ell$, the verifier also derives
\begin{equation}
i=(b\ll w)\mid j,\qquad
\omega_i^{(\ell)}=\omega_b^{(\ell)}2^j,\qquad
 y_i^{(\ell)}=g^{\omega_i^{(\ell)}}.
 \label{eq:chunk-exponent-data}
\end{equation}
These exponents define one successor PESAT claim for each active chunk:
\begin{equation}
 \bigwedge_{\ell\in\mathcal A}
 \RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z}^{(\ell)},\mathcal C,\Pack).
 \label{eq:chunk-pesat-family}
\end{equation}
\subsection{The encoded F2Z PESAT relation}

Each accepted chunk asserts one grand-product identity.  For one active chunk,
write its residual polynomial as
\begin{equation}
 Q_c^{(\ell)}(\Bbit)
 :=\prod_i\left(1+(y_i^{(\ell)}-1)\Bbit[c,i]\right)
   -\nu_c^{(\ell)}.
 \label{eq:chunk-pesat-polynomial}
\end{equation}
The encoded F2Z PESAT relation collects the equations
$Q_c^{(\ell)}(\Bbit)=0$ for every column:
\begin{align}
 &\RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z}^{(\ell)},\mathcal C,\Pack)
 \notag\\[-2pt]
 &\quad:=
 \Bigl\{((\bm y^{(\ell)},\bm\nu^{(\ell)}),O;\Bbit):
 \Bbit\in\Ftwo^{2^s\times2^d},\ 
 O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(\Pack(\Bbit)),\ 
 Q_c^{(\ell)}(\Bbit)=0\ (\forall c)\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-gp}
\end{align}
Omitting $\ell$ gives the unchunked notation
$\RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z},\mathcal C,\Pack)$.  Thus the grand-product claim is
represented by this encoded PESAT relation.

Checking all products directly would be expensive.  GKR instead compresses
their verification into a linear claim on the same committed bits.

\paragraph{Direct reductions and classifications.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Reduction 1: Linearize with GKR.}
\begin{equation}
 \RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z},\mathcal C,\Pack)
 \xRightarrow{\Pi_{\rm red}}
 \RLin(\K,\mathcal C,\Pack).
 \label{eq:edge-gkr-reduction}
\end{equation}
For the chunked family, this reduction runs once per $\ell\in\mathcal A$.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Input:} the chunk's PESAT instance, the unchanged root
 $\rho=\Root(O)$, and the transcript after $\zeta^{(\ell)}$ and
 $e_0^{(\ell)}$; call this bundle $\mathcal I_{\rm GKR}^{(\ell)}$.
 \item \textbf{Proof:}
 \begin{equation}
  (\tr',\mathit{pt}_\ell,\mu_\ell)
  \leftarrow
  \mathsf{GKR.Verify}
  (\mathsf{pp}_{\rm GKR},\mathcal I_{\rm GKR}^{(\ell)},
   \pi_{\rm red}^{(\ell)};\tr).
  \label{eq:gkr-verifier-contract}
 \end{equation}
 This call consumes exactly one canonical GKR fragment
 $\pi_{\rm red}^{(\ell)}$ and returns the updated transcript $\tr'$.  The GKR
 protocol fixes its messages, challenge order, encodings, tags, and rejection
 rules.
 \item \textbf{Result:} the verifier derives $(\bm a_\ell,\mu_\ell)$ for
 $\RLin(\K,\mathcal C,\Pack)$, retaining the same $O$ and bit witness.  In the
 current proof system, with
 $B_{\mathrm{bit},\K}:=\iota_{2\to\K}(\Bbit)$ coordinatewise,
 \begin{equation}
  \bm a_\ell[z]=\eq(z,\mathit{pt}_\ell),
  \qquad
  \MLE{B_{\mathrm{bit},\K}}(\mathit{pt}_\ell)=\mu_\ell.
  \label{eq:gkr-lin-successor}
 \end{equation}
 \item \textbf{Scope:} this edge assumes a GKR instantiation with a byte-exact
 transcript and a proof of the reduction.  Its error is
 $\varepsilon_{\rm red}^{(\ell)}$ per active chunk.
\end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Classification: Degree-zero, zero-ideal PIMSAT.}
With $S=\K[X]$, $S_{\le0}=\K$, and $I=(0)$, membership means exactly
$Q_c^{(\ell)}=0$.  Boolean witnesses keep their bit domain; unrestricted
$\K$ variables also require $Z^2-Z=0$.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{The encoded LIN relation}

After GKR, neither the source constraints nor the grand products remain.
The encoded LIN relation records the sole remaining linear claim.  Using the
coordinatewise embedding defined above, put
$\bm b_{\K}:=\iota_{2\to\K}(\pi_2(\bm b))$:
\begin{align}
 \RLin(\K,\mathcal C,\Pack):=
 \Bigl\{((\bm a,\mu),O;\bm b):\;&
 \bm b\in\bitsset^n,
 \quad\bm a\in\K^n,
 \quad\mu\in\K,\notag\\[-2pt]
 &O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(\Pack(\pi_2(\bm b))),
 \quad\langle\bm a,\bm b_{\K}\rangle_{\K}=\mu\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-lin-pack}
\end{align}
This is the linear target consumed by the opening IOP.

For the fixed geometry $n=2^m$, write $\widetilde a$ and
$\widetilde b_{\K}$ for the multilinear extensions of $\bm a$ and
$\bm b_{\K}$.  The direct GKR successor is the special case
$a[z]=\eq(z,r)$, so its claim is already
$\widetilde b_{\K}(r)=\mu$.  A general coefficient need not have this form.
The final reduction now opens the derived linear claim against the original
root.

\paragraph{Direct reductions.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Reduction 1: Run the linear-relation IOP.}
\begin{equation}
 \RLin(\K,\mathcal C,\Pack)
 \xRightarrow{\Pi_{\rm iopp}}
 \mathcal R_{\rm triv}.
 \label{eq:edge-iopp}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Proof:} In Equation~\eqref{eq:edge-iopp}, $\pi_{\rm iopp}$
 proves the derived $(\bm a,\mu)$ claim against the public root
 $\rho=\Root(O)$.
 \item \textbf{Result:} acceptance or rejection; no public relation remains.
 \item \textbf{Scope:} the final direct edge.  The construction below gives one
 concrete implementation.
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Fixed opening protocol's internal expansion of
  \texorpdfstring{$\Pi_{\rm iopp}$}{Pi-iopp}}

The fixed recursive-opening protocol implements $\Pi_{\rm iopp}$ as follows.
Direct MLE-shaped claims proceed to the ring switch, while virtual claims first
pass through the LinCheck below.
\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{One LinCheck for a general coefficient.}
The direct mode skips this item.  For a general claim
$\langle\bm a,\bm b_{\K}\rangle_{\K}=\mu$, the prover and verifier run the
degree-two sumcheck for
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{x\in\bits^m}\widetilde a(x)\widetilde b_{\K}(x)=\mu.
 \label{eq:lincheck-sum}
\end{equation}
Starting with $\sigma_{-1}:=\mu$, round $i$ sends one degree-two polynomial
$g_i$ and enforces
\begin{equation}
 g_i(0)+g_i(1)=\sigma_{i-1},\qquad
 r_i\leftarrow\K,\qquad \sigma_i:=g_i(r_i),\qquad 0\le i<m.
 \label{eq:lincheck-round}
\end{equation}
These equations fix every round check and challenge deadline.  Let
$r=(r_{\lo},r_{\hi})$, where $|r_{\lo}|=7$.  The first ring-switch message
derives
\begin{equation}
 z:=\sum_{v=0}^{127}\eq(v,r_{\lo})s_v
    =\widetilde b_{\K}(r),\qquad
 \sigma_{m-1}=\widetilde a(r)z.
 \label{eq:lincheck-terminal}
\end{equation}
The verifier checks Equation~\eqref{eq:lincheck-terminal} immediately after
absorbing that message and before the next squeeze.  This terminal rule uses
no division and has no exceptional branch when $\widetilde a(r)=0$.  The
LinCheck proof fragment is $\pi_{\rm LC}:=(g_i)_{i=0}^{m-1}$ and its
soundness error is at most
$\varepsilon_{\rm LC}:=2m/|\K|$.  After the check, the same $s$ vector is
reused by the ring switch; no second LinCheck or extra evaluation target is
introduced.

\item \textbf{Per-claim ring switch.}
For $0\le v<128$, define the $v$-th bit plane of a packed row by
$B_{\mathrm{bit},v}[c,i_{\hi}]
:=\Bbit[c][(i_{\hi}\ll7)\mid v]$, using the coordinate order from
Equation~\eqref{eq:packed-commitment}.  For each active $\ell$, the prover
transmits
\begin{equation}
 s^{(\ell)}=(s_v^{(\ell)})_{v<128},
 \qquad
 s_v^{(\ell)}:=\MLE{B_{\mathrm{bit},v}}(r_{\hi}^{(\ell)}).
 \label{eq:ring-switch-message}
\end{equation}
After all vectors in Equation~\eqref{eq:ring-switch-message} are absorbed,
the verifier squeezes one shared $r''$ and derives a packed claim
\begin{equation}
 O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(P),
 \qquad
 \langle B_\ell,P\rangle_{\K}=\beta_\ell.
 \label{eq:packed-claim}
\end{equation}
In Equation~\eqref{eq:packed-claim}, the witness is
$P=\Pack(\pi_2(\bm b))$ and $\rho=\Root(O)$ is unchanged.  This step
has error $\varepsilon_{\rm ring}^{(\ell)}$.  For a direct MLE-shaped source,
the verifier checks $z=\mu_\ell$; for the single general claim normalized by
LinCheck, it checks Equation~\eqref{eq:lincheck-terminal}.  Thus the ring
switch itself still consumes only one MLE evaluation.

\item \textbf{Batch.}
The verifier squeezes fresh
$\bm\eta\in\K^{|\mathcal A|}$ and derives, without a proof field,
\begin{equation}
 B:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal A}\eta_\ell B_\ell,
 \qquad
 \beta:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal A}\eta_\ell\beta_\ell.
 \label{eq:batch-packed-claim}
\end{equation}
The batch values in Equation~\eqref{eq:batch-packed-claim} define the
resulting internal claim
$O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(P)$ and
$\langle B,P\rangle_{\K}=\beta$, with batching error
$\varepsilon_{\rm batch}$.

\item \textbf{Recursive opening.}
The prover transmits $\pi_{\rm open}=\pi_{\rm lig}$, one fixed
recursive constrained-code proof containing its internal proximity messages
and authenticated openings.  The verifier derives all opening challenges and
accepts only if the proof verifies against $(\rho,B,\beta)$.  This completes
$\Pi_{\rm iopp}$ with error $\varepsilon_{\rm open}$.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The virtual Lin-BitsDual extension}

The virtual extension provides an optional route into the same LIN relation
when the claimed values come from a public linear transformation of the
committed bits.

For a public $\Ftwo$-linear map $T:\Ftwo^n\to\Ftwo^{n'}$ and
$h_0\in\Ftwo^{n'}$, let
$\operatorname{lift}_2:\Ftwo\to\{0,1\}\subset\Z$ act coordinatewise and put
$\bar h=h_0+T(\pi_2(\bm b))$.  Define
\begin{align}
 \RLBD^{\virt}:=\Bigl\{((T,\bm a_q,\mu,h_0),O;\bm b):\;&
 \bm b\in\bitsset^n,
 \quad\bm a_q\in\Fq^{n'},
 \quad\mu\in\Fq,\notag\\[-2pt]
 &O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(\Pack(\pi_2(\bm b))),
 \quad
 \langle\pi_q(\operatorname{lift}_2(\bar h)),\bm a_q\rangle_{\Fq}
 =\mu\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-lbd-virtual}
\end{align}
This is the source relation for the virtual branch.

\paragraph{Direct reductions.}
\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Conditional virtual reduction.}
\begin{equation}
 \RLBD^{\virt}
 \overset{\Pi_{\rm partial\text{-}core}}{\dashrightarrow}
 \mathcal R_{h\text{-}{\rm Lin}}^{\virt}.
 \label{eq:edge-virtual-partial-core}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Proof:} For the conditional edge in
 Equation~\eqref{eq:edge-virtual-partial-core}, $\pi_{\rm virtual}$ carries
 the chosen partial-core and GKR messages; the virtual table is not
 transmitted.
 \item \textbf{Result:} the verifier derives $(\bm a_{\K},\mu')$.
 \item \textbf{Scope:} available only after the virtual mode defines the
 proof format, transcript, verifier, and error bound.
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

Let $h_{0,\K}:=\iota_{2\to\K}(h_0)$ and let $T_{\K}$ be the scalar extension
of $T$.  Only $\bm b$ is committed.  The virtual linear seam is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal R_{h\text{-}{\rm Lin}}^{\virt}:=
 \Bigl\{((T,h_0,\bm a_{\K},\mu'),O;\bm b):\;&
 \bm b\in\bitsset^n,
 \quad\bm a_{\K}\in\K^{n'},
 \quad\mu'\in\K,\notag\\[-2pt]
 &O=\Enc_{\mathcal C}(\Pack(\pi_2(\bm b))),\notag\\[-2pt]
 &\langle\bm a_{\K},h_{0,\K}+T_{\K}(\bm b_{\K})\rangle_{\K}
 =\mu'\Bigr\}.
 \label{eq:rel-virtual-lin}
\end{align}
This relation is the partial core's required successor.  Its public adjoint
rewrite transmits nothing:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal R_{h\text{-}{\rm Lin}}^{\virt}
  &\equiv_{\mathsf{Adj}_{T_{\K}^{*}}}
  \RLin(\K,\mathcal C,\Pack),\\
  \bm a_b&:=T_{\K}^{*}(\bm a_{\K}),&
  \mu_b&:=\mu'-\langle\bm a_{\K},h_{0,\K}\rangle_{\K}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:virtual-adjoint-rewrite}
\end{equation}
The exact rewrite in Equation~\eqref{eq:virtual-adjoint-rewrite} need not
preserve the direct mode's MLE coefficient shape.  The virtual mode therefore
invokes the LinCheck in
Equations~\eqref{eq:lincheck-sum}--\eqref{eq:lincheck-terminal} before the
existing ring switch.  It must provide a deterministic evaluator for the
adjoint coefficient, for example by the dual identity
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{T_{\K}^{*}(\bm a_{\K})}(r)
 =\left\langle\bm a_{\K},T_{\K}(\eq(\cdot,r))\right\rangle_{\K},
 \label{eq:virtual-adjoint-evaluator}
\end{equation}
The virtual profile must prove that this evaluator equals the declared full
adjoint.  No dense adjoint vector and no multi-evaluation ring adapter are
required.  A virtual profile is admissible only if it specifies the
partial-core predecessor, evaluator, LinCheck ledger, and composed error.
Otherwise the verifier rejects; direct mode always rejects this source.

\subsection{The complete relation graph and proof object}

Collecting the preceding reductions gives the complete relation graph.  Along
the direct path, Bitify changes the witness view once, from $\bm f$ to
$\bm b$, while the encoded oracle and root remain fixed.  Let
$\mathcal R_{\rm triv}:=\{(\bot,\bot;\bot)\}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_{\rm src}
 &\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Encode}}
 \mathcal R_{\rm src}^{\rm enc},
 \\[1pt]
 \mathcal R_{\rm src}^{\rm enc}
 &\xRightarrow{\Pi_{\rm ZC}}
 \RLMFB,
 \\[1pt]
 \RLMFB
 &\equiv_{\mathsf{Bitify}}
 \RLBD
 \xRightarrow{\Pi_1}
 \RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z},\mathcal C,\Pack)
 \\[1pt]
 \RPESAT(\K,Q_{\Ftwo\!\Z},\mathcal C,\Pack)
 &\xRightarrow{\Pi_{\rm red}}
 \RLin(\K,\mathcal C,\Pack)
 \xRightarrow{\Pi_{\rm iopp}}
 \mathcal R_{\rm triv}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:solid-reduction-graph}
\end{equation}
$\mathsf{Encode}$ and Bitify add no proof fields; chunking and recursive
opening refine the displayed edges.  Concatenating the messages carried by the
interactive edges gives the semantic proof object
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{\mathrm{F2Z}}
 =\left(
   \pi_{\rm ZC},
   \bigl(\nu^{(\ell)},u^{(\ell)},\pi_{\rm red}^{(\ell)}\bigr)_{
         \ell\in\mathcal A\text{ increasing}},
   \pi_{\rm LC},
   (s^{(\ell)})_{\ell\in\mathcal A\text{ increasing}},
   \pi_{\rm lig}
 \right).
 \label{eq:proof-object}
\end{equation}
The fields in Equation~\eqref{eq:proof-object} are replayed according to
Section~\ref{sec:interaction}.  Public
inputs are external to the proof; other omitted values are derived.  The v1
wire format partitions transmitted fields, in replay order, as
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{\rm wire}:=(N_{\rm F2Z},H_{\rm F2Z}),\qquad
 \begin{aligned}
  \NargLen(\pi)&:=|N_{\rm F2Z}|,&
  \HintLen(\pi)&:=|H_{\rm F2Z}|,\\
  \PayloadLen(\pi)&:=|N_{\rm F2Z}|+|H_{\rm F2Z}|,&
    \WireLen(\pi)&:=
    |\HostEncode(N_{\rm F2Z},H_{\rm F2Z})|.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:proof-channel-lengths}
\end{equation}
Benchmarks \must{} report all four lengths.  Section~\ref{sec:wire-container}
fixes their byte meaning, the outer 40-byte container, and the exact record
counts and exhaustion rules.

With ZeroCheck error $\varepsilon_{\rm ZC}$,
$\varepsilon_{\rm red}^{(\ell)}$, the optional $\varepsilon_{\rm LC}$,
$\varepsilon_{\rm ring}^{(\ell)}$,
$\varepsilon_{\rm batch}$, and $\varepsilon_{\rm open}$,
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_{\rm F2Z}
 \le \varepsilon_{\rm ZC}
    +\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal A}
       \bigl(\varepsilon_{\rm red}^{(\ell)}
             +\varepsilon_{\rm ring}^{(\ell)}\bigr)
    +\varepsilon_{\rm LC}
    +\varepsilon_{\rm batch}+\varepsilon_{\rm open}.
 \label{eq:composed-soundness}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:composed-soundness} is the proof system's composed soundness
bound.  The direct mode sets $\pi_{\rm LC}=()$ and
$\varepsilon_{\rm LC}=0$.
